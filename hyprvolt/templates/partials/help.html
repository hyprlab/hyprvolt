Everything here is a record: a server, a VLAN, a service, a room, a contract. What makes it more than a list is how the records connect, and there are three ways they do.

Where it is

A record's Location puts it in a site, building, room or rack. That is a place, not a dependency: a server in Rack 1 doesn't stop working when you rename the rack. The place lists it in its Contents tab.

Links

A link says how two records relate: a VM runs on a hypervisor, a service depends on a database, a UPS powers a switch. Links are in a record's Relationships tab, and some fields and form sections make them for you (a UPS's Powers, a switch's Networks).

{% if net %}
Cables

A cable is the physical connection: from a port on one device, perhaps through a patch panel, to a port on another. Cables are in a device's Cabling tab and the setup guide's Cables step.

{% endif %}
What follows from them

From the links that carry a dependency{% if net %}, and from the cables{% endif %}, Hyprvolt works out what each record needs and what breaks if it goes down. You never record that twice: link a VM to its host{% if net %} and cable the host to a switch{% endif %}, and the {% if net %}switch{% else %}host{% endif %}'s "What breaks" lists the VM.

{% endcall %} {% call pane('guide') %}

The site setup guide documents a site a step at a time, in the order a network is built: the place, the network, the equipment, what runs on it, the endpoints, and the cables between them.

Each step is a list of rows, one record a row. Type a name in the blank row and press Enter to add it; change any field of a row in place, and it is saved as you leave it. The What goes here button beside a step's title says what belongs in it.

{% if net %}

The steps build on each other. The Network gear step says which VLANs and subnets each switch carries; the Cables step uses that, and where each device is, to suggest the cabling (Suggest cables) and to label each cable by its network, such as 20-03 for the third cable on VLAN 20.

{% endif %}

{% if site_guide %}Open it from Set up a site in the sidebar. {% endif %}It can be run again for another site, or to add what was left out.

{% endcall %} {% call pane('links') %}

A link joins two records and reads both ways: "vm1 runs on pve1" is "pve1 runs vm1" from the other end.

Adding one

In a record's Relationships tab, Add link opens the form. Choose how this record relates to the other one, then the record, and an optional note. Removing a link asks first, and can be undone.

Which links carry a dependency

Only some kinds of link mean one record stops working when the other goes down. Those are the ones followed to answer "What breaks".

{% endcall %} {% if net %} {% call pane('cables') %}

A cable joins two devices, port to port. Cables live in each device's Cabling tab and in the setup guide's Cables step.

Ports

A device is cabled as a whole until you record its ports one by one; a cable then goes to a named port. A switch's Ports count, if set, is how many cables it takes as a whole.

Patch panels

A patch panel's front and rear ports are paired. A trace goes in at one and out at the other, so a cable from a desk jack through the panel to a switch traces from end to end.

MoCA and wireless bridges

A pair of MoCA adapters, or of wireless bridges, works like one cable: a device on the far end reaches the switch on the near end over the coax or the air. Each adapter is still its own record, with its own place and its own UPS.

Labels

A cable added in the setup guide without a label is given one from its network: 20-03 is the third cable on VLAN 20, UP-01 an uplink between network gear, WAN-01 from the modem.

Do I link what is cabled?

No. A cable already counts as a dependency, worked out each time from the cables as they are:

  • A device cabled to network gear (a switch, router, firewall, modem or MoCA adapter) needs it, through any patch panels on the way.
  • Between two pieces of gear, the one further from where the internet comes in needs the nearer one: an edge switch needs the core switch, the core needs the firewall, the firewall needs the modem.
  • Two devices cabled to each other at the same distance, such as a server and a NAS on a direct link, need nothing of each other.
  • The far end of a MoCA or wireless bridge pair needs the near end.
  • A UPS or a peripheral, such as a PDU, is managed over the network and goes on working without it, so its cable makes it need nothing.

Each device's Relationships tab lists what it is cabled to, read-only, under Cabled to. Change the cable in the Cabling tab and the dependency follows. Add a link only to say something a cable can't, such as a service that depends on a database.

{% endcall %} {% endif %} {% call pane('breaks') %}

Every record's Relationships tab ends with two questions: what breaks if this goes down, and what it needs.

How it is worked out

From the record, Hyprvolt follows every link that carries a dependency{% if net %}, and every cable{% endif %}, step by step. If a switch goes down, so does the server plugged into it, the hypervisor that runs on the server, every VM on that, and every service on those VMs.

The view opens as a drawing, what it needs above the record and what breaks below. List shows the same as nested lists. A record reached two ways is drawn once, and a loop is marked rather than followed.

In the drawing, the − on a record folds away what hangs from it, and the +N left in its place shows it again. Collapse all keeps only the records next to this one. The folds last until the record is closed.

{% if 'maintenance' in help.modules %}
Maintenance windows

A maintenance window that affects a record lists, under Also goes down, everything that breaks with it, worked out the same way.

{% endif %}
When something is missing

If a dependency you expect isn't there, one link in the chain is: a VM with no Runs on, a service with no Depends on{% if net %}, a server with no cable to its switch{% endif %}. Add it where it belongs and the whole chain follows.

{% endcall %} {% if net %} {% call pane('networks') %}

The network is recorded from where the internet comes in, outward.

Where the internet comes in

An internet line is the modem, router or firewall it comes in at, not a record of its own: its Internet line section holds the circuit ID, speeds and any static address, and its Supplier is the ISP. Everything cabled behind that device goes down with it, and the network diagram draws it first.

VLANs, subnets and addresses

A subnet can belong to a VLAN. A device's IP addresses place it in a subnet, which is how Hyprvolt knows which network a device is on when it labels a cable or suggests a switch.

A site of several networks

When a building has its own internet line, router and addresses, choose that building as the Where of its VLANs and subnets. Its devices' addresses are then in its subnets, so two buildings can both use 192.168.1.0/24 without a clash.

VPN tunnels

A VPN is a network of the kind VPN whose Ends are the routers, firewalls or servers it runs between, such as each building's firewall. The diagram draws it between them, and it goes down with either end.

Which switch carries which network

A switch, router or firewall lists the VLANs and subnets it carries in its Networks section. A network then depends on the gear that carries it, so a switch's What breaks lists its networks.

Wireless networks

A wireless network is broadcast by access points, extenders, bridges and Wi-Fi routers, ticked from either record.

{% endcall %} {% endif %} {% if hw %} {% call pane('power') %}

A UPS's Powers section ticks the equipment plugged into it.

Each one is a Powered by link, which carries a dependency: if the UPS fails, everything it powers goes down{% if net %}, along with whatever is cabled behind it{% endif %}. The setup guide has a UPSes step for this, after Endpoints.

{% if net %}

Each end of a MoCA pair, or of a wireless bridge, is its own record, so each can be powered by its own UPS.

{% endif %} {% endcall %} {% endif %} {% call pane('records') %}

A record shows all of its sections, one after another, Overview first.

Changing it

An editor changes a field where it is shown: point at it or click it, type, and it is saved as you leave it. A change that can't be saved says why under the field.

Deleting and archiving

Deleting a record can be undone from the message that follows, and it waits under Recently deleted until it is purged. Archiving keeps it, with its history and links, out of lists and search.

History

Every change, link and cable is written to the History of the records it touches, with who made it.
